**Agent:**

"Couples the stories" means one story's content depends on another story's state. Here's how that would play out in the party scene.

If story 3 showed the ghosts from stories 1 and 2 at the party, it would have to check whether the user had already played those stories. That raises three problems:
- **Order:** a user who plays story 3 first would see a party with no guests, or we'd have to show everyone anyway, which defeats the payoff.
- **State:** the game would need to remember what has been seen, and replays and pause/resume would have to keep that state correct. That's more code and more places for bugs.
- **Testing:** instead of 3 stories to verify, there would be 3 stories plus the orderings that change what you see.

If the stories stay independent, each one plays the same way every time, whatever else has happened. The shared sheet ghost still ties them together as a recurring character. Each story is then simpler to build, test and replay, and the brief requires all three.

Coupling isn't wrong, just costlier. A cheap middle ground would be a small "ghosts found: 2/3" counter in the corner. It would show progress without changing what any story plays. I'd treat that as a possible later extra, not part of the first build.
